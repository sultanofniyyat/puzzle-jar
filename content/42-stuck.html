Every Puzzle Jar level can be won with no help: a computer solver beats each one before it ships. When you are stuck you have three helpers shared by all 20 games: a hint shows a safe move, a take back undoes your last move (3 free on every level), and a rescue gives you a little extra room after you get stuck. New players start with 3 hints, 3 take backs and 1 rescue, and losing a level never costs anything.

What are hints, take backs and rescues?

They are the three helpers in Puzzle Jar, each with its own count. A hint lights up a good next move, worked out by the game's solver. A take back puts your last move back. A rescue is the "so close" save once you're stuck: an extra tray hole, jar, shelf, a few more moves or hearts, depending on the game. Each game's own page says what its hint and rescue do.

How many hints do you start with?

Three hints, plus 3 take backs and 1 rescue, as a welcome gift on your first launch. That is enough to try each helper before you ever need more. On top of that, the first 3 take backs on every level are free and do not touch your count, so your saved take backs are only used from the fourth one on a level.

Is there a limit on hints per level?

No. You can use as many hints on one level as you have in your count, one at a time. Each hint shows only the next safe move, not the whole answer, so on a long level you may want several. Hints are shared by all 20 games, so the count you see in Sweet Stacks is the same one you'll spend in Honey Hive.

How do take backs work in Puzzle Jar?

Each take back undoes one move, and the first 3 on every level are free. The button shows "3 FREE", then "2 FREE" and so on; after that each take back comes from your count. The free three reset on every new level and every retry. Sweet Stacks goes further: its undo and restart are always free. With Remove Ads, take backs cost nothing in every game.

What is a rescue in Puzzle Jar?

A rescue is a second chance that appears on the stuck screen when a level is almost lost. It changes the board just enough to carry on: in Bolts & Jars a tray hole, in Bead Swap 5 extra moves, in Quilt Pins 3 more hearts, in Route Duel a replay from a few moves back against a weaker rival. In every game except Route Duel you keep all the progress you made before getting stuck.

How many rescues can I use on one level?

Two in most games, one in four of them. Sweet Stacks, Nut Sort, Pencil Case and Shelf Sort allow a single rescue a level, because their rescue (an extra jar, bolt, refilled hearts or shelf) is strong enough to turn almost any position around. In the other games a second rescue is allowed. Past the cap, the stuck screen offers a take back, TRY AGAIN and HOME instead.

Is the first rescue on a level free?

In most games it can be, if you watch a short video. The stuck screen offers the first rescue of a level for a rewarded video, and a second one from your rescue count. You never have to take the video: you can pay with a rescue you own instead, or skip the rescue and start the level again for free.

How do I get more hints for free?

Two ways, both without paying. Keep winning, because wins fill the jar on the Home screen, which pays out one of each helper (more on the jar below). Or watch a rewarded video from the offer sheet, which gives +1 of the helper you asked for. Today's puzzles count as wins for the jar too, so the dailies help fill it.

What happens when I run out of hints?

An offer sheet opens, the same in every game. It lists the packs for that helper with prices in your currency, a "WATCH AD" button for one free, and "NOT NOW". Choosing NOT NOW closes the sheet and returns you to the board as it was; nothing is lost and the level carries on without the hint.

What does "WATCH AD · +1" mean?

It means one short video gives you one of the helper you were trying to use: one hint, one take back or one rescue. You get it only if the video plays to the end; closing it early gives nothing and costs nothing. The helper goes into your shared count, so you can spend it in this game or any other. Videos need an internet connection.

What is the jar on the Puzzle Jar home screen?

It's a progress jar that fills as you win. Every win in any of the 20 games drops a piece into it in that game's color, and the next road level is shown beside it. When 10 pieces are in, the jar is full: you get one hint, one take back and one rescue for any game, and a fresh, empty jar starts.

Are Puzzle Jar levels rigged to make you pay?

No. Each level is fixed: the same pieces, colors and order every time you play it, and in games where new pieces fall in, the order they fall is set for that level too. Nothing changes when you are close to winning, and the game never deals a worse board because you have hints or money to spend. If a level beat you, the same level, unchanged, is waiting when you retry.

Is every Puzzle Jar level possible to win?

Yes. We run a solver over each level before it goes in the app, and it must finish the level with zero helpers. Levels the solver cannot clear are rebuilt or dropped. Quilt Pins goes one step further: every quilt has exactly one answer that can be reached by thinking alone, with no guessing.

Does the game get harder if I keep winning?

Yes, a little. Puzzle Jar follows how you play, which we call the dial. Two clean wins in a row, with no retry and no hint or rescue, move you a step harder. In 18 games that means the next levels come from a tougher set of the same level numbers; Quilt Pins moves up its own ladder, and in Route Duel the rival plays stronger.

Does Puzzle Jar get easier if I keep losing?

Yes. A win that needed a retry or a rescue moves the dial a step easier, so the next level comes from a gentler set. It goes back up once you string clean wins together again. In Route Duel, losing a duel makes the computer rival play weaker. The level you are stuck on itself never changes, though: the dial only picks the next one.

Why does the first level of the day feel easier?

Because it is, by one step. The first new level you play each day is chosen one step gentler than your dial would otherwise pick, so you start the day with a win in reach rather than a wall. From the second level on, the dial goes back to its normal setting.

What are easy, medium and hard rounds?

Levels come in rounds of three: easy, medium, hard. On the level road you play one round of a game, then move to the next game, round and round through all 20. An easy level always follows a hard one, so a tough level is followed by a breather. Levels 1 to 5 of each game are only easy or medium.

What is a boss level?

Every fourth hard level is a boss: the toughest level of its stretch in that game, and in some games the place where a new obstacle shows up at its strongest. When you beat it, the win screen says "Boss beaten". A boss sits at the end of a round, so the next level is always an easy one.

Does a level change when I retry it?

No. Once a level number has been picked for you, it stays the same level until you move past it, however many times you retry. You can learn it, plan from the first move and use what went wrong last time. The dial only adjusts the levels after the one you are on.

What should I do when a level feels impossible?

Try these in order:

  • Use your 3 free take backs to go back to the last point where you had choices.
  • Start again and watch the first few moves; most hard levels are lost early, not at the end.
  • Take one hint to see a safe move, then carry on yourself.
  • Take a break: play the day's puzzles or another game in Free play and come back.

Every level has a winning path, and retries are free.

Can I skip a level in Puzzle Jar?

No, there is no skip button. A level on the road has to be won before the road moves on. When you are stuck, the stuck screen offers a rescue (while one is still allowed on that level), a take back, TRY AGAIN and HOME. You don't have to stay on it, though: every game is open in Free play, so you can play something else and come back.

Can I play a different game when I'm stuck?

Yes. Go HOME and pick any of the 20 games from Free play; they are all open from the first launch. Free play moves that game's own level forward, while your road level waits where you left it. Wins in Free play still fill the jar, which can give you a free hint for the level you were stuck on.

Does Puzzle Jar get harder forever?

No, it levels off. In 18 of the games, levels grow over the first 150 on the road, adding pieces, colors and obstacles at set points; after level 150 such a game carries on by replaying from its level 51, so the difficulty settles in its middle and hard range instead of climbing without end. Quilt Pins draws from a bank of 2,000 quilts, and Route Duel has 340 picture boards.

What happens when I fail a level?

Nothing is taken from you. There are no lives to lose, no coins and no timer before you can play again. The stuck screen offers a rescue where one is allowed, TRY AGAIN to replay the same level from the start, or HOME. A full-screen ad may come before the next screen once you have finished 3 levels, unless you own Remove Ads.

Can you lose level 1?

No. Level 1 of every game is a guided level that cannot be lost: a glow shows you where to tap while you learn the rules. In Route Duel, level 1 is a guided first duel. Losing becomes possible from level 2, and even then the first five levels of each game are only easy or medium.

Do hints make the next level harder?

No. A win with a hint or a rescue is not counted as a clean win, so it never moves the dial toward harder levels. A rescue or a retry actually moves it a step easier. Take backs don't count against you at all, since three a level are free. Use helpers when you want; the game won't punish you for it.

Why is one game in Puzzle Jar harder than another?

Because each game keeps its own dial. If you breeze through Hop On but struggle in Bead Swap, Hop On moves to its tougher levels while Bead Swap moves to gentler ones. The road also saves the two thinking-heaviest games, Quilt Pins and Route Duel, for last in each lap. Our puzzle types page explains what each kind of game asks of you.

Is there a walkthrough or answer list for Puzzle Jar levels?

No, and a fixed list would not help much. In 18 of the games each level is picked from a normal, gentler or tougher set depending on how you have been playing, so your level 40 may not be the same board as a friend's level 40. The hint button is the walkthrough: it shows a safe next move worked out by the same solver that checked the level before release, on exactly the board you are looking at.
