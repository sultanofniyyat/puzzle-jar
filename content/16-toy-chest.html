Toy Chest is the color block jam in Puzzle Jar. Painted wooden blocks sit on a grid inside a toy chest, and the chest's walls have colored doors. Drag a block and it slides through empty squares; when it reaches a door of its own color that is wide enough, it slides out. Get every block out to win. There is no clock, only a number of moves, and each drag uses one.

How do you play Toy Chest?

Put your finger on a block and drag it toward a door of the same color. It moves through empty squares only, never through another block or a stone. Once it touches its door, it shoots out of the chest. The counter at the top shows how many blocks are left and how many moves you have.

Why won't my block go out through the door?

Usually one of two reasons. The door is not its color, or the door is too slim for the block. Doors are one to three squares wide, and a block only leaves through a door at least as wide as the side it is pushing through. A three-wide block needs a three-wide door of its color, or a different way round.

Does every drag in Toy Chest cost a move?

Yes, one drag is one move, however far the block slides. That is the whole dial of the game: hard levels give just enough moves for a good plan. A long slide across the chest costs the same as a one-square nudge, so make each drag count.

How do you lose in Toy Chest?

You run out of moves with blocks still in the chest, or no block can move at all. Then the blocks wiggle red and the game offers a rescue, a fresh try or home. Since there is no clock, you can take as long as you like over each drag.

What are the gray stones in Toy Chest?

Fixed obstacles. Stones arrive at Toy Chest level 7, never move and never leave; blocks have to go round them. They often turn a short exit into a longer path, so look for them before you plan a block's route.

How do two-color blocks work?

A two-color block has an outer color and an inner one. Slide it to a door of the outer color and that layer peels off; the same block, now in its inner color, then needs a door of that color. Two-color blocks appear from level 16. Each one takes at least two moves, so count them twice when you plan.

What do ice, one-way blocks and locks do?

  • Ice (from level 31): a frozen block cannot move until a set number of other blocks have left the chest.
  • One-way blocks (from level 46): they slide only across, or only up and down.
  • Locks (from level 61): locked blocks open once the block carrying the key has left.

Each new obstacle gets a short card the first time it shows up.

What is the best strategy for Toy Chest?

Find the blocks that are in everyone's way and plan their exits first. A block that can leave right now is rarely the wrong move, but check where it goes, because a block parked in front of another door costs you a move later. On levels with locks or ice, the key block and the blocks that thaw the ice come first.

What does the Toy Chest hint show?

The next move of a win the solver found within the moves you have left. The game keeps that plan, so as long as you follow the hints, they never lead you astray. If you take your own route halfway, the next hint plans again from where you are.

What does the Toy Chest rescue do?

Two things at once: it gives you five more moves, or enough to bring every block left out with two to spare, whichever is more, and it lets you lift any one block straight out of the chest. You choose the block, so pick the one jamming everything. A level allows two rescues.

How hard does Toy Chest get?

Level 1 has three blocks, a glowing guide and cannot be lost. Grids grow from 5 by 5 to 8 by 8 and blocks from one to six squares. New obstacles join one at a time, from stones at level 7 to locks at level 61. Every fourth hard level is a boss, like the one in the picture, and an easy level always follows a hard one.

Why does Toy Chest count moves instead of time?

Because a clock punishes slow thinkers, and puzzles should reward thinking. Countdown clocks are a common complaint about color block jam games, so we left the clock out and limited moves instead. You can stop mid-level and think for as long as you like; the only pressure is the move count.
