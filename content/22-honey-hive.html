Honey Hive is the hexa sort game in Puzzle Jar. You drag stacks of six-sided honey tiles onto a honeycomb; when a stack lands next to one with the same color on top, those tiles hop over and pile up together. Ten or more of one color on top are collected, and you win by collecting the number of tiles the honey bar asks for. There is no clock and no move limit: the only way to lose is a comb with no free cell left.

How do you play Honey Hive?

Drag one of the three stacks in the tray onto any empty cell of the comb. Each stack holds 2 to 6 tiles in one to three colors, and only the color on top matters for merging. If a stack in a touching cell shows the same color on top, those tiles hop across one by one. Once all three tray stacks are placed, three new ones are dealt.

Why did my tiles keep hopping after I dropped a stack?

One drop can start a chain. After tiles hop onto the stack you placed, the stack they left shows a new color on top, and if that color matches one of its own neighbors, it pulls tiles in turn. Collected tiles do the same thing. Looking at what lies just under each top layer is how you predict a chain, and with practice, how you set one up on purpose.

What does the number on a Honey Hive stack mean?

It counts the tiles of one color sitting on top of that stack, down to the first tile of a different color. A stack showing 8 needs only 2 more of its color to reach 10 and be collected. Tiles lower down in other colors don't count until everything above them is gone.

How do you win a level of Honey Hive?

Collect the number of tiles the honey bar asks for, for example "0 of 80 tiles collected" at the start of a level. Tiles are collected when 10 or more of one color sit on top of a stack: they burst and fly to the bar, and whatever was underneath stays on the cell, or the cell empties. Goals run from 20 tiles on level 1 to as many as about 160 on the biggest boss levels, and most levels take about two minutes.

How do you lose in Honey Hive?

The comb fills up. When every open cell already holds a stack and a stack from the tray has nowhere to go, the stacks shake and you are stuck. You can then rescue, take back a drop, try the level again or go home. Taking your time never counts against you, because nothing counts your drops.

Where should I place a stack in Honey Hive?

Beside the stacks whose top color it matches, and away from colors it would block. A stack with red on top dropped between two blue stacks stops both of them from merging until the red is gone. Push messy, many-colored stacks toward the edges and keep the middle of the comb for merges, since a middle cell touches six others and an edge cell touches fewer.

How many empty cells should I keep free?

As many as you can, and never count on the last one. All three stacks in the tray need a home before new ones arrive, so a comb with one or two free cells can be lost even when it looks fine. Placing really matters here: when we tested a player who drops stacks anywhere, it won about 2% of hard levels and no boss levels at all.

What are the dark locked cells in Honey Hive?

Cells you can't use yet. Each one shows a number, the tiles you still need to collect before it opens, and it counts down as you play. They first appear at level 12. A cell that opens gives you room exactly when the comb is getting crowded, so collecting early also buys space. No cell in Honey Hive is ever opened by watching an ad.

What are iced stacks?

Stacks frozen in place on the comb, from level 25. An iced stack takes no part in merges until 10 tiles are collected on a cell right next to it, which cracks the ice. They build on an idea from level 5, where some levels start with stacks already on the comb, so you plan around them from your very first drop. Each new piece gets a short card the first time it shows up.

Which stack and cell does the Honey Hive hint pick?

It shows which tray stack to drop and on which cell. To pick it, the game tries every empty cell for every stack in the tray and plays the six best choices through the rest of the tray before it decides. Because each level deals the same stacks in the same order every time, a hint means the same thing on a retry as on the first try.

What does the bee rescue do?

Bees fly in and carry off the most mixed-up stacks on the comb, between 2 and 6 depending on the level, which clears room to keep going. Each level allows two. The first is offered for an optional video; if you skip that or want a second, it is paid from your stock of rescues. On hard and boss levels we set the number of stacks lifted so that at least 70% of stuck players can still win.

Can I undo a drop in Honey Hive?

Yes. Take back puts the last stack back in the tray and reverses the hops and collections it caused. You get three free take backs on every level. Since the deal never changes, you can try a cell, watch the chain, undo it and test another cell, which is a good way to learn how chains work.

Do Honey Hive levels get harder as you go?

Gentle at first, then properly tough. Level 1 has 19 cells and two colors and can't be lost. Hard levels shrink the comb to 12-16 cells with six colors, and boss levels use up to seven. On a hard level, between about a third and two thirds of our computer test players get stuck on the first try, and easier levels always follow the hard ones.

Is Honey Hive a hexa sort game?

Yes. It plays by the familiar hexa sort rules: three stacks offered at a time, matching tops merge, 10 of a color clear, and a full board ends the run. In Honey Hive, locked cells open only through play; each level's deal is fixed, so it is the same puzzle every try; and every level was won by our solver with no hints or rescues before it went in the game.
