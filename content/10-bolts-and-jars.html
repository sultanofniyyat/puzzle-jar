Bolts & Jars is the screw puzzle in Puzzle Jar and the first game on its level road. Colored plates are stacked on top of each other and held down by screws. You tap screws to take them out, each screw flies into a jar of its own color, and you win when every jar is full. There is no clock and no move limit: you only lose if the waiting tray fills up and no screw can go anywhere.

How do you play Bolts & Jars?

Tap a screw to unscrew it. If a jar of the same color is open, the screw flies straight in. If not, it waits in the tray at the bottom, which has 5 holes. Two jars are open at a time, and the game shows which jar comes next. Three screws fill a jar; a full jar leaves and the next one slides in.

Why won't a screw come out when I tap it?

A plate lying on top of it is holding it in. In Bolts & Jars a screw can only leave when nothing above it covers its hole. Take out the screws of the plate on top first; once that plate has no screws left, it drops away and frees the screws under it.

How do you win a level?

Fill every jar. When the last jar is full, the board is clear and the level is won. The number of jars is the real size of a level: early levels have only a few, and levels grow by about one jar per round of three.

How do you lose in Bolts & Jars?

Only one way: the 5-hole tray is full and none of the screws you can reach matches an open jar. Then the game stops and offers a rescue, a fresh try or the way home. Running slowly never loses; you can stop and think for as long as you like.

What is the best strategy for the screw puzzle?

Look before you tap. The jars tell you which colors are wanted now and next, so take screws of those colors first and leave the others where they are. A screw left on the board costs nothing; a screw sent to the tray takes one of only five holes. Treat the tray as emergency space, not a waiting room: on hard levels, a tray that fills early is the usual reason a run ends.

Which plate should I clear first?

Usually the top plate that is hiding the most screws. Freeing one big plate can open three or four screws at once, which gives you choices. Clearing a small corner plate first often leaves you with nothing useful to tap.

What do the symbols on the screws mean?

Each color has its own mark: a heart, a shield, a star, a tick, a bolt or an eye. The mark always goes with the same color, so you can play by shape alone. We added it so that players who find some colors hard to tell apart are never stuck guessing.

What does a hint do in Bolts & Jars?

A hint makes one screw glow. It is a screw that is safe to take now, worked out by the same solver we use to check our levels, so it never walks you into a dead end. Hints are shared by all 20 games, so one spent here is one less for the others.

Can I undo a move in the screw puzzle?

Yes. Take back puts your last screw back where it was. The first three take backs on every level are free; after that each one comes from your take-back count. With Remove Ads bought, take backs are unlimited.

What is the "+1 hole" rescue?

When the tray is full and you are stuck, the rescue adds one more hole to the tray so you can keep going. You can use it up to twice on a level. The first rescue of a level can come from a short video you choose to watch; the next one uses a rescue from your count.

Are Bolts & Jars levels possible without hints?

Yes. Every level is played through by a computer solver before it goes in the game, with no hints and no rescues, so a winning order of taps always exists. Hard and boss levels are meant to make you think; they are not set up to force a purchase.

How hard does Bolts & Jars get?

It starts gentle: level 1 shows you where to tap and cannot be lost. Levels come in rounds of easy, medium and hard, and every fourth hard level is a boss. The game also follows how you play: two easy wins in a row make the next level a step harder, and a level you needed a retry or rescue for makes the next one a step easier.

Is Bolts & Jars a good screw puzzle for adults?

We think so, and adults are who we made it for. There are no timers, no lives to wait for and no reading. It suits people who like to plan a few moves ahead and enjoy the click of a full jar, more than people who want speed or action.
