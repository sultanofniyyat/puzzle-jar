Mosaic Tray is the block puzzle in Puzzle Jar. You drag pieces of glazed tiles onto an 8 by 8 wooden tray, and any row or column filled from end to end clears. Some tiles hold jewels; clear the lines they sit in to collect them, and collect every jewel to win. Unlike endless block games, each level has a set number of pieces, dealt in a fixed order, so every level plays the same way each time and can be won.

How do you play Mosaic Tray?

Drag a piece from the three under the tray and drop it where it fits. While you drag, the spot it would land on shows faintly and any lines it would complete glow. Fill a row or column and it clears. When you have placed all three pieces, the next three are dealt. The jewel counter at the top shows how many are left to free.

How do you collect the jewels in Mosaic Tray?

Complete the row or the column a jewel tile sits in. When that line clears, the jewel flies up to the counter. Each jewel starts in its own row and column, so you choose which way to free it: across or down. Pick whichever line is closer to full.

Can you rotate pieces in Mosaic Tray?

No. Pieces go on the tray the way they are dealt. That is part of the planning: a long upright piece only fits a column gap, so keep space for the shapes you can see coming rather than counting on turning them.

Why does Mosaic Tray have a piece limit?

Because it turns the tray into a puzzle with an answer. The pile next to the jewel counter shows how many pieces the level gives, from 9 to 45. Endless block games deal at random, and a common complaint about them is a hand that cannot fit however well you play. Here the order is fixed and our level maker has already won with exactly that pile.

How do you lose a level of Mosaic Tray?

Either none of the pieces in your hand fits anywhere on the tray, or the pile runs out while jewels are still waiting. The hand shakes red and a card offers a rescue, a take back, a new try or the way home. There is no clock.

What is a good Mosaic Tray strategy?

Work toward the jewels, not toward tidy lines. Clearing a row with no jewel in it feels good but spends pieces. Keep the middle of the tray open, because the bigger pieces need room, and fill toward edges and corners first. Before you drop a piece, check whether it blocks the only spot where the next large shape could go.

Does clearing several lines at once help in Mosaic Tray?

Yes, in two ways. A double or triple clear frees more of the tray for the pieces still to come, and it can collect two jewels with one piece. Clears made with pieces in a row also show "Combo x2" and up. The combo words are for fun; what matters is the space and the jewels.

What does the hint do in Mosaic Tray?

It shows a safe place for one of your pieces: a spot from which the level can still be won with the pieces left. Before showing it, the solver plays the rest of the level through. If no win is left from where you are, no hint is charged, which is your sign to take back or try again.

What does Take back undo in Mosaic Tray?

It lifts the last piece off the tray, and if that piece cleared lines, the tiles and jewels in those lines come back too. That makes it safe to test an idea: drop a piece, see what it leaves, and lift it off again. Each level gives three take backs for free.

What does the Mosaic Tray rescue give you?

Room to move. Your stuck hand is swapped for single tiles, three more singles follow, and ten extra pieces are added to the pile. Single tiles fit any empty square, so they can finish an almost-full row. You can rescue up to twice on one level.

Is Mosaic Tray like other block puzzles?

It uses the same 8 by 8 board and row-and-column clears as the popular endless block puzzles, but every level is a set puzzle with jewels to collect and a fixed pile of pieces. You will not get a run ruined by a random bad hand. If you prefer endless high-score play, that is the one thing Mosaic Tray does not offer.

How hard does Mosaic Tray get?

Level 1 has two jewels and a glowing guide on each turn, and cannot be lost. From there, levels come in rounds of easy, medium and hard. Hard and boss levels put more jewels on the tray, deal fewer small pieces and more awkward ones, and leave little or no spare in the pile, so expect to think a few pieces ahead.
