Button Tin: the triple tile match

By the Sultan of Niyyat team, who make Puzzle Jar. Last updated 2026-11-24.

Button Tin is the triple tile match game in Puzzle Jar and the third game on the level road. Sewing buttons lie in a pile, up to five layers deep, inside a tin. You tap free buttons onto a card with seven spaces, three of a kind are sewn together and leave, and you win when the tin is empty. There is no clock and no move limit; the only danger is a full card.

How do you play Button Tin?#

Tap any button that has nothing lying on top of it. It jumps down to the button card under the tin and sits next to others of its kind. As soon as three alike are on the card, they are sewn together and disappear, which frees their spaces. A counter at the top shows how many buttons are still in the tin.

Why can't I tap some buttons in Button Tin?#

They are covered. A button is free only when no other button overlaps it from above, even by a small edge; covered buttons are drawn shaded so you can tell them apart. Take the buttons lying on top first and the one underneath lights up. On most levels you can still see a covered button's kind through the shade, which is useful for planning.

How do you win Button Tin?#

Empty the tin. Every kind of button comes in sets of three, so if you keep making threes, the last button taken always completes the last set. The counter at the top falls to zero and the tin is shown empty. Early levels have only a handful of buttons; later piles hold dozens.

When are you stuck in Button Tin?#

When all seven spaces on the card are filled and no three of them match. The game then shows a "Card full!" message with your ways out: add one more space to the card, take back a few buttons and try another order, start the level again, or go home. Nothing else ends a level, so slow, careful play is never punished.

What is the best strategy for Button Tin?#

Treat the card as seven spaces you can lose, not seven you can use. Every kind on the card that is not yet a pair is a space at risk, so the safest buttons to take are ones that make a three or turn a single into a pair. Taking a new kind when the card already holds four or five different ones is how most runs end. Count before you tap.

Which buttons should I take first in Button Tin?#

First, any free button that completes a three already on the card. Next, buttons that uncover the most others, often the ones near the top of a deep stack, because every button you reveal is another choice later. Since covered buttons are shaded but still show their kind on most levels, look for a pair hiding under one free button before you take it.

What are face-down buttons in Button Tin?#

Buttons that show only a question mark while something lies on them, so you cannot see their kind until they become free. They come in from level 31 on hard and boss levels. On those levels you lose the ability to plan from shaded buttons, so keep an extra space free on the card for surprises.

What do the symbols on the buttons mean?#

Each kind of button has a color and a matching symbol, such as a heart, a shield, a star, a tick, a lightning bolt or an eye. The pair never changes, so you can match by symbol alone if two colors look similar to you. On a busy pile, the symbol is often the quickest way to spot a third button.

What does a hint do in Button Tin?#

A hint makes one free button glow, and taking it keeps the level winnable. It is the first move of a winning order found by the solver we use to check every level, so it never leads you into a full card. Each hint uses one from the count shared by all 20 games, and you can use as many as you have.

What does take back do in Button Tin?#

It lifts the last button off the card and puts it back in its old place in the tin, where it covers the same buttons as before. The first three take backs of every level are free, and after that each one uses your shared take-back count. The full-card message offers it too, because stepping back two or three buttons is often enough.

What is the +1 Space rescue?#

It gives the card one more space, eight instead of seven, so a full card has room to finish a three. You can use it up to twice on one level. The first time on a level you can earn it with an optional video; the second uses a rescue from your shared count.

How hard does Button Tin get?#

Level 1 has nine buttons of three kinds and cannot be lost. Piles then grow slowly, to roughly 70 to 96 buttons, with more kinds and more layers, and boss levels add more buttons, an extra layer and one more kind on top of a normal hard level.

Is Button Tin like a mahjong or triple tile match game?#

It is a triple tile match: like mahjong solitaire, you can only take pieces that are free, but here you collect three of a kind on a card instead of pairing two. If you enjoy tile matching games where you clear a stack into a tray of seven, Button Tin plays the same way, with buttons, a shown count of what is left and no timer.
