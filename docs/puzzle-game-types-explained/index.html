Puzzle game types explained

By the Sultan of Niyyat team, who make Puzzle Jar. Last updated 2026-11-24.

Most phone puzzle games belong to a small set of genres: sorting (screws, nuts, water, goods, yarn), matching (triple tile, match-3), placing (block puzzles, hexa sort), clearing (bus jam, tap-away, tap-to-blast, bubble shooter) and pure logic (Star Battle grids). Below, each genre is explained in plain words, with the Puzzle Jar game that plays it. The second half covers why these games have ads, whether levels are rigged, and what research says, with sources, about puzzles, the brain, stress and sleep.

What is a screw puzzle game?#

A screw puzzle is a board of plates or planks held down by colored screws, and you take the screws out to clear it. Most versions add a rule about where a removed screw goes: into a box, jar or tray of its color, with a small spare space for screws that have nowhere to go yet. The thinking is about order: which screw to free first so the spare space never fills. In our Bolts & Jars, a screw is held in until the plate lying over it is gone.

What is a nut sort game?#

A nut sort game is a stacking puzzle: colored nuts are threaded on upright bolts, and you move the top nuts from bolt to bolt until each bolt holds a single color. You may only drop nuts onto the same color or an empty bolt, so the empty space is what you manage. It is a close cousin of water sort, with metal parts instead of liquid. Our Nut Sort mixes tall bolts that hold 5 with short helper bolts that hold 2 or 3.

What is a color sort or water sort puzzle?#

It is the genre where tubes or jars hold mixed layers of color and you pour or lift the top layer into another container until every container holds one color. A layer can only land on its own color or in an empty container, so you need a free spot to shuffle with. Ball sort is the same idea with balls. In Puzzle Jar this genre is Sweet Stacks, played with sweets in jars, where four alike earn a lid.

What is a triple tile match game?#

A triple tile game deals a pile of picture tiles, some covering others, and a short holding bar underneath. You tap tiles that are not covered; each one moves to the bar, and three matching tiles in the bar disappear. If the bar fills with no three alike, the level ends. It grew out of mahjong-style tile matching, but you match three instead of pairs. Our version, Button Tin, uses buttons in up to 5 layers and a card with 7 spaces.

What is a match-3 game?#

Match-3 is the swap-neighbors genre: you switch two pieces next to each other so that three or more of one color line up and clear, and new pieces drop in from above. Levels usually set goals, such as clearing blockers, within a fixed number of moves. Bigger lines make special pieces that clear rows or areas. Bead Swap is Puzzle Jar's match-3, played with beads, where a swap that makes no line slides back and costs nothing.

What is a block puzzle game?#

In a block puzzle you place shaped pieces on a square grid, and any row or column you complete clears away. Pieces cannot be turned, and the game ends when the pieces you hold no longer fit anywhere. Many block puzzles are endless high-score games with random pieces. Ours, Mosaic Tray, is a level-based kind: an 8x8 tray, a set number of pieces in a fixed order, and jewels you collect by clearing the lines they sit in.

What is a color block jam puzzle?#

It is a sliding-block puzzle where colored blocks fill a walled board and each block can leave only through an exit of its own color. You drag a block in a straight line; it slides until something stops it. The challenge is the order: which blocks to move aside so the right one reaches its door. It is related to classic parking and unblock puzzles. In Toy Chest each drag counts as one move, and later boards add ice, one-way blocks and locks.

What is a bus jam game?#

A bus jam game shows a crowd of colored passengers and a stop where buses of one color arrive in turn. You tap a passenger who has a clear way out; they board the matching bus, or wait on a small bench if their bus is not there yet. A full bench with no one able to board ends the level. Some versions use cars in a parking lot instead. Our Hop On always shows which buses come next and has no clock.

What is a tap-away puzzle?#

A tap-away puzzle fills a board with pieces that each point one way, and tapping a piece sends it sliding out in that direction. If another piece is in its path, it bumps and stays. You clear the board by working out which piece is free first, then which ones that frees. It rewards looking before tapping more than speed. In Pencil Case the pieces are pencils that leave tip first, and each bump costs one of 3 hearts.

What is a yarn sort or wool game?#

Yarn games are a recent family of sorting puzzles built around wool: you unwind balls or knitted figures, and the yarn travels to a spool or basket of its color, with a small waiting area for colors not wanted yet. They play like a screw or bus puzzle with soft materials, and the look of wool unspooling is a big part of the appeal. Snug Skeins is ours: felt toys lose one layer of wool per tap.

What is a goods sort game?#

Goods sort games put small items, such as drinks, snacks or toys, on rows of shelves, and you move them until three of the same item stand side by side and vanish. Items hidden behind the front row move forward as shelves empty, so part of the skill is planning for what you cannot fully see yet. Space is the danger: if every slot fills, you are stuck. Shelf Sort is our goods sort, with 12 kinds of goods that each have their own outline.

What is a merge drop game?#

In a merge drop game you drop round objects into a container from the top; two identical ones that touch join into the next, larger kind. Real physics makes things roll and settle, and you lose if the pile rises over a line at the top. The well-known example uses fruit and ends with a watermelon, so people often call the genre "watermelon games". Treat Drop is a level version: each level asks you to make certain sweets before your supply runs out.

What is a hexa sort game?#

Hexa sort means stacking puzzles played on a honeycomb of six-sided cells. You place small stacks of colored hexagon tiles; when neighbors share a top color, tiles hop from one stack to the other, sometimes in long chains. Once enough of one color pile up together, they clear. Because every cell has six neighbors, one placement can set off many moves, which is the genre's draw. Our hexa sort is Honey Hive, where 10 or more of a color on top are collected.

What is a tap-to-blast game?#

Tap-to-blast (also called collapse or blast games) clears groups instead of lines: you tap two or more touching pieces of one color and the whole group pops. Pieces above fall into the gaps, and large groups leave behind rockets or bombs. Unlike match-3, nothing has to be swapped. Puzzle Jar has three of them: Fruit Tumble (fruit falls down), Balloon Bunch (balloons float up) and Tide Pool, where new rows push up from below every few taps.

What is a bubble shooter?#

A bubble shooter is an aiming game: a field of colored bubbles hangs at the top, and you fire one bubble at a time from the bottom. Hitting a group so that three or more of a color touch pops them, and any bubbles left hanging by nothing drop off. Bouncing shots off the side walls is a core skill. Many older ones push the ceiling down over time. Bubble Picnic, ours, shows the full path of each shot and never lowers the ceiling.

What is a rope untangle game?#

A rope untangle game gives you a knot of ropes or wires pinned between posts, and you move the ends until no rope crosses another. It is a puzzle about crossings, so the trick is spotting which end blocks the most others. The idea is old: free logic collections have long included a version with dots and lines. Our Garland Tangle uses strings of lights; a string glows once it crosses nothing, and each level gives a set number of moves.

What is a Star Battle puzzle?#

Star Battle is a pencil-and-paper logic grid: a square board is split into irregular regions, and you place stars so each row, column and region holds the set number of stars. Well-made grids are meant to be solved by reasoning rather than guessing. LinkedIn's Queens is a one-per-region variant (LinkedIn help). Quilt Pins is ours: one pin in every patch, row and column, on quilts from 4x4 to 11x11.

What counts as a puzzle game?#

A puzzle game is one where the main challenge is working something out, such as an order, a placement or a path, rather than reflexes or luck. On phones the label also covers relaxed sorting and matching games that some players would call "casual". A useful test: if a slow, careful player does better than a fast one, it is a puzzle. By that test, 19 of Puzzle Jar's 20 games are pure puzzles; the twentieth, Route Duel, adds a computer rival.

What are sorting games good for?#

Mostly for a calm kind of focus. A sorting game gives you a messy board and one clear goal, so for a few minutes your attention has a simple job and visible progress. They also exercise planning: on hard levels you must think a few moves ahead to keep a free spot. For young children, sorting by color and shape is a familiar early-learning activity, though a phone game is no substitute for real toys. We make no claim beyond that; the research on puzzles is covered further down this page.

Why are sorting games so satisfying?#

Designers usually point to order appearing out of mess, and to small, certain rewards. The makers of the tidying game Unpacking described its core as "the joy of unboxing things", and compared playing it to popping bubble wrap (Film Stories). A 2021 study of puzzle players found they kept going when the game met their need to feel capable, in control or connected, and quit easily when it frustrated those needs (Pusey, Wong and Rappa, Games and Culture). Claims about "dopamine hits" from sorting are popular but we found no study that tests them.

What is the difference between hyper-casual and hybrid-casual games?#

Both are simple to learn, but they are built and paid for differently. Unity's glossary describes hyper-casual games as quick builds earning mostly from banner and full-screen ads, while hybrid-casual games add a longer progression layer, earn more from purchases and opt-in reward videos, and are "built to engage players for 60-90 days" (Unity; Unity sells ad technology, so read it as an industry view). Many sorting puzzles today, Puzzle Jar included, sit in the hybrid group: a level road, helpers and optional videos.

Why do mobile puzzle games have so many ads?#

Because almost all of them are free to download, and ads pay for the free players. On Google Play, AppBrain counts 96.7% of all apps as free (AppBrain, all apps, not only games). One 2025 industry report put 32.2% of mobile game revenue from in-app ads and 67.8% from purchases (XMP and Insightrackr, via PocketGamer.biz). Puzzle games have simple, short levels, which makes a natural pause for an ad. How we handle ads is on our free and ads page.

Why do mobile game ads look nothing like the game?#

Some advertisers show a puzzle or scene that the game barely contains, because it gets more taps. UK regulators have ruled against this: in 2020 the Advertising Standards Authority found pin-pulling ads for two well-known home-decorating games misleading even with a "not all images represent actual gameplay" note (ASA ruling, 2020), and in November 2026 it upheld in part a complaint about a big match-3 game's ad (ASA ruling, 2026). If an ad promises a game, check the store screenshots before you install.

Are mobile puzzle games rigged?#

It depends on the game, and the honest word is "tuned" more than "rigged". Some games deal random pieces, so a run can turn unwinnable through bad luck. Others change difficulty based on how you play: researchers at Electronic Arts published a system that adjusts difficulty to keep players playing longer (Xue et al., 2017). In Puzzle Jar each level is fixed and proved winnable by a solver, but which level you get next does follow how you play, and Route Duel's rival plays stronger or weaker. Details are on our difficulty page.

What makes a puzzle level fair?#

In our view, three things. First, it must be winnable without help; we check every Puzzle Jar level with a computer solver before it ships. Second, what you need to decide should be visible or fairly hinted, such as the next jar or the next bus, so a loss comes from a choice rather than a hidden coin flip. Third, it should play the same on a retry, so you can learn from losing. A level can be very hard and still fair; it is unfair when skill cannot change the result.

Do brain training games make you smarter?#

The evidence says you get better at the games, not at thinking in general. A major 2016 review found "little evidence" that training improves performance on distantly related tasks (Simons et al., Psychological Science in the Public Interest), and a 2010 online study in Nature found no transfer to untrained tasks (Owen et al.). In 2016 the US Federal Trade Commission made the maker of Lumosity pay $2 million over brain-training claims it said lacked science (FTC). Enjoy puzzles for what they are.

Can puzzle games prevent dementia?#

No game has been shown to prevent dementia. The Alzheimer's Society says "there is no strong evidence" that brain training reduces risk (Alzheimer's Society). The closest trial result, from the ACTIVE study, involved a specific speed-of-processing training with booster sessions, not puzzle games, and the link was modest (Coe et al., 2026). The 2024 Lancet Commission estimated that about 45% of dementia cases are potentially preventable by tackling 14 risk factors, including hearing loss, physical inactivity, high blood pressure and social isolation (Livingston et al.). Those are better places to start.

Do puzzles keep your memory sharp as you get older?#

People who do puzzles often do tend to score better on thinking tests, but that does not prove the puzzles caused it. The PROTECT study of about 19,000 adults aged 50 and over found that "frequency of word puzzle use is directly related to cognitive function" (Brooker et al., 2019), and a companion paper from the same team reported a similar link for number puzzles. The Alzheimer's Society noted such studies "can only show that puzzling and thinking skills are linked" (Alzheimer's Society). Puzzles are a pleasant habit, not a proven treatment.

Can a puzzle game help with stress?#

For short spells, some research says it can. In a 2019 study, students waiting for stressful news who played Tetris at a level that absorbed them felt better than those whose game was too easy or too hard (UC Riverside). A 2020 review of 13 studies said casual games "may have promise" for stress and low mood (Pine et al.), but the studies were small and short. A game is not a treatment; the NHS advises seeing a GP if you are "struggling to cope" with anxiety (NHS).

Is Tetris really used after trauma?#

In research, yes, but in a specific way. In a trial published in 2017, people in a hospital emergency department after a car crash recalled the event and then played about 20 minutes of Tetris; they had fewer intrusive memories in the first week, but the trial did not show wider benefits at one month (Iyadurai et al., Molecular Psychiatry). An earlier lab study found a similar drop in flashbacks (Holmes et al., 2009). The authors called for larger trials. It is a research method with a set routine, not a reason to self-treat with any game.

Do puzzle games help you sleep?#

There is no good evidence that they do, and screens before bed are usually advised against. The NHS insomnia advice says not to use devices "like smartphones, right before going to bed" (NHS), and the American Academy of Sleep Medicine suggests powering down electronics 30 to 60 minutes before bed (AASM, 2026). If you do play in the evening, a game with no clock and no "one more life" countdown is easier to put down; ours has Reduce motion and sound switches in Settings.

Are "win real money" sort and tile games legit?#

Be careful with them. Some are real skill-gaming apps with published rules, and many others are ordinary puzzle games whose ads promise cash that is hard or impossible to collect. Before you spend time or money, read the payout terms, check whether you must deposit money to "cash out", and read the low-star reviews, which is where payout problems show up. Puzzle Jar does not pay money or prizes of any kind; there is nothing to cash out, and nothing in the game is worth real money.
